# MECHANICS MAP — what the eyes actually get

A plain-language tour of every visual mechanic in the piece, described the way
you'd describe the wall to someone who can't see it: motion, light, texture,
tempo, feeling — not math names. Written so we can **find new ways to unlock
things**. The last section (§8) is the point: which shapes still have no unlock,
and the mechanic each one is quietly asking for.

Firewall-safe: everything here is about light and motion, nothing about anyone.

Companion docs: `SHAPE-CATALOG.md` (the maths), `LIGHTSHOW-PLAYBOOK.md`
("woah dude" scoring), `INDEX.md` (which file draws what).

---

## 1. The master motion — what "resolving" feels like

Almost everything does one thing, over and over: it starts as **scatter** and
pulls itself into **order**, holds for a breath, then loosens back into scatter.
Picture TV static slowly gathering itself into a clean face, holding, then
dissolving again. That gather-and-hold is the heartbeat of the whole piece.

- **Scattered** feels restless, grainy, searching.
- **Resolved** feels like a held chord — everything suddenly agreeing.

This single dial is called σ (chaos → symmetry). **Every unlock in the piece is
just a reason to turn it.** A game never fakes a reward — it takes the *same*
shape from scatter to order as you win. So a new unlock doesn't need a new
picture; it needs a new reason to gather.

## 2. The spirals — the calm family

The slow, hypnotic ones. A **line of light that keeps turning as it grows**,
winding outward from the centre like water going down a drain in reverse, or the
arms of a galaxy. Some are tight and clockwork-even; some open up loosely the
further out they go. A few grow in little jumps — a bead lands, then another,
hopping around a ring until, without warning, all the beads reveal they were
sitting on a perfect fan the whole time (the sunflower: the gaps suddenly look
*right* and curving lanes pop into being). Patient, breathing — the 2am stare.

*Includes: logarithmic, Euler, Theodorus, Doyle, involute, Sacks, Ulam, Fermat,
Recamán, phyllotaxis, spherical spiral.*

---

## 3. The roses and woven curves — the lace family

A pen tied to two swinging pendulums. The light **loops back through itself
again and again**, laying petal on petal until it's a dense flower or a piece of
spinning lace. Perfectly symmetrical, mirror on mirror. Some look like a
spirograph toy — a gear scribbling a flower that closes exactly where it started.
When two frequencies *lock*, a wobbling tangle snaps into a clean standing
shape. That snap is very satisfying.

*Includes: rose curve, Maurer rose, mystic rose, Lissajous, harmonograph,
times-table cardioid, spirograph, guilloché, lemniscate, hypocycloid, epicycloid
gears, star polygon, roots of unity.*

---

## 4. The fractals — the "fall in forever" family

These don't spin; they **have depth**. A coastline that, leaned into, is made of
smaller coastlines, made of smaller ones still, with no bottom. A fern built
entirely of tinier ferns. A snowflake edge that keeps sprouting more edge. The
motion is a slow *dive* — the image zooms toward one point and there's always
more detail arriving, like falling into a well with no floor. Cool, mathematical,
a little vertiginous.

*Includes: Mandelbrot, Julia, Newton fractal, Koch snowflake, dragon curve,
Hilbert curve, Sierpiński carpet, Barnsley fern.*

---

## 5. The attractors and chaos — the smoke family

A single point of light **left loose in the room**, drawn along an invisible
current. It never repeats but it never leaves — it keeps tracing the same
butterfly-shaped or looping region, like cigarette smoke that somehow always
coils into the same ghost. Alive, unpredictable, weather-like. You can feel it
*almost* about to settle, then it swerves.

*Includes: Lorenz, Aizawa, Thomas, Hopalong, chaos game, flow field.*

---

## 6. The living-skin family — cellular & reaction

Fields that **spread and eat themselves**. Spots grow, bump, split like
something under a microscope; ripples chase ripples; blobs drift together and
fuse like lava-lamp wax. Colours bleed and react at their edges. Less a picture,
more a *surface that's alive* — coral, bacteria, oil on water.

*Includes: cellular automaton, Game of Life, reaction-diffusion, Truchet tiles,
plasma, metaballs, Chladni plate.*

---

## 7. The organisms — creatures that live *on top* of the spiral

The big move. Over whatever shape is showing, a **living thing is drawn in
glowing light**, and you can dial how much of it is present.

- **Synaptic** — a web of nodes with **sparks running along the threads**, like
  a nervous system firing. Pulses travel node to node; now and then a wave of
  light washes across the whole web. Electric, twitchy, blue-white.
- **Slime mould** — real crawling. Thousands of tiny agents **grow veins toward
  food**, thickening the paths they use and abandoning the rest, until you see a
  branching network of glowing tubes — the mould that solves mazes. Wet,
  organic, reaching.
- **Superfluid** — **light floods through channels** like liquid poured into a
  riverbed; the nodes swell into soft blooms and breathe. Gentle, weightless,
  watery.
- **Murmuration** — flocks of little agents that **suddenly all turn the same
  way at once**, like starlings at dusk — clumping, swirling, breaking apart.
- **The Wiring Network** — cells drift, **throw out feelers, touch, and lock
  into wiring**, then a tangle of separate colours jumps into one connected
  lattice. Its trick is colour: every cell is born a different hue and *keeps*
  it, so colour **bleeds one step at a time along the wires** — fierce at a seam
  where two colours meet, barely moving at the far tips. A living rainbow
  gradient that never turns to mud.
- **"Me Julie"** — the ferrofluid. A **black spiked membrane, like a sea urchin
  made of oil**, thorns that stiffen and ride the bass. Rough, physical, a bit
  menacing — the odd one out among all the light.

Two ways an organism meets the shape underneath:

- **Placement** — instead of sitting in the middle, the creature **spreads out
  to frame the edges and corners**, leaving the centre open and lit.
- **Possession** — the creature can **feel the spiral as terrain and feed on
  it**: slime crawls the bright curves, the flock traces the arms, blooms fire
  where the shape is brightest. Picture and creature stop being two layers and
  become one thing.

## 8. Colour, breath, and the pendulums

- **Palette** — the whole room wears one of a set of named colour moods (and can
  invent new ones on the fly). This colour also goes out to the physical lamps,
  so screen and room match.
- **The swing** — the entire image **rocks slowly back and forth**, like
  something seen through gently tilting water. A busy room rocks harder, faster.
- **The zoom** — the picture **dives into its own centre and pulls back out**,
  timed against the gather-and-hold so it's deepest right at resolution (or the
  opposite, if flipped).
- **The held breath** — at each far end of the swing it **pauses**, a moment,
  before turning back. That pause is the tension.

---

## 9. The two ways to fuse shapes

- **Mash** — braids two shapes' actual motion: one shoves the other around, or
  they melt halfway into a true hybrid, or little copies of one get planted all
  along the body of the other.
- **Stardust blend** — the universal one. Any two shapes shatter into **clouds
  of glowing dust that migrate**, one shape flowing across the room and
  re-forming as the other. Even the flat, sealed images join in — everything can
  become sand and pour into everything else.

---

## 10. The garden — "I made that"

When someone plays and wins, **their result is planted into the live room** —
their spiral, at the exact angle or setting they chose, appears on the big
screen, breathing, and stays there.

## 11. THE UNLOCK GAP — the brainstorming payload

There are 66 shapes. **16 have a game; ~50 do not.** Everything above is a
reason to gather σ; here's where the reasons are missing, and the mechanic each
family is quietly asking for.

| Family with no unlock | What's waiting | The mechanic it suggests |
|---|---|---|
| **Fractals** | Mandelbrot, Julia, Newton, Koch, dragon, Hilbert, Sierpiński, Barnsley | *dive deeper* — the reward is falling further in; a "go deeper" or path-trace game |
| **Attractors** | Lorenz, Aizawa, Thomas, Hopalong, flow field | *steer the loose point*, or tune 2–3 knobs until the ghost shape emerges |
| **Living skin** | Life, cellular automaton, reaction-diffusion, Truchet, plasma, metaballs | *seed it and keep it alive* — tend a growing surface; tile-matching |
| **3D / composed** | torus, torus knot, icosahedron, galaxy, mandala | *rotate-to-align*, fold, orbit until it locks |
| **Number theory** | Sacks, Ford circles, roots of unity, times-table | *pick a number / hunt a ratio* — the sunflower's golden-angle feel, reused |
| **Harmonic curves** | Lissajous, harmonograph, spirograph, guilloché, lemniscate | *tuning* — dial two frequencies until the wobble snaps into a clean shape |

**Three unlock verbs that fit the σ contract cleanly:**

1. **Tuning games** — the shape only resolves when two ratios are right, so σ
   *is* your closeness. (The sunflower already proved this feel.)
2. **Discovery games** — you don't "solve," you *find*: nudge a parameter and
   watch a smoke-shape emerge; the reward is a threshold, not a win screen.
3. **Non-game unlocks we half-have** — the feelings quiz (steer a shape into
   being), the crowd mechanics (many people → one geometry), and neglect (a
   shape earned by leaving the house *alone*, or by a room filling up).
