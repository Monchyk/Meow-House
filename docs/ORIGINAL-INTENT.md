# ORIGINAL INTENT — what Deep House was born to be

Plain-language account of the installation's founding idea, distilled from the
design north star (`DESIGN.md`, the brief K. endorsed 2026-07-12). Not a plan,
not status — the *why* the whole thing exists, kept in one readable place.

Firewall note: this describes the concept. It talks about the origin the way the
brief does — *one person's lived experience* — never naming or accusing anyone.
The rules in §7 are the reason it can.

---

## 1. The one-sentence idea

**A house you can walk through that behaves like a living nervous system** — an
interactive detective-noir art installation where TV displays and real Philips
Hue lights react to emotional state, so every feeling physically changes the
room. Inspired by one person's lived experience (autism, ADHD, alexithymia,
masking, burnout, justice sensitivity, sensory processing) — but it **never
diagnoses, never lectures, never blames. It is art. Everything is discovered,
nothing is explained.**

## 2. The reversal — the key insight

The audience won't spend 30 minutes reconstructing someone's inner world up
front. So the ramp is turned around:

> **fun → mysterious → beautiful → (only then) revealing.**

"Come solve a weird mystery in a haunted smart house" → "wait, the house behaves
like a nervous system" → "oh — I've been reconstructing one person's inner
experience this whole time." The emotional depth is always present but **scales
with the audience** without changing the concept. Someone who only ever thinks
"cool lights, weird puzzle" still has a complete, successful experience. *(The
"Portal rule": the depth is a second layer, never a toll gate.)*

## 3. The four phases a visitor moves through

1. **Curiosity** — the house is strange, lights and music react, nothing is
   explained, everything is playful.
2. **Investigation** — connecting clues: cold cases, emotion fragments, colours,
   math patterns.
3. **Empathy** — they realise the environment behaves like a nervous system;
   nothing changed except their *interpretation*.
4. **Integration** — the mystery was never "what happened" but "why did this
   feel so different inside than it looked outside." The ending is **peaceful,
   not victorious.**

## 4. The house is a character

It breathes, remembers, gets overwhelmed, calms, and sometimes **masks** —
hiding clues to protect itself. It never behaves like a computer; it behaves
like a living nervous system. Behind it, one hidden **operator** performs that
nervous system in real time — tuning invisible variables (masking, trust,
cognitive load, sensory overload, dopamine, regulation, fatigue, heartbeat) and
never narrating. The visitor only ever experiences the *consequences*, so every
run is unique.

## 5. The visitor is an intervention, not a tourist

The founding non-negotiable: **the visitor always has agency.** Never a pretty
haunted house where things merely happen *to* them. Calming a room requires
*action*; restoring a memory requires *reconstruction*; repairing a pattern
requires *cooperation*; unlocking empathy requires *observation*. And there is
**no game-over** — failure is emotional, not a loss: the house becomes
defensive, clues distort, and *another path opens.* A misunderstanding is
content, never a dead end.

## 6. The two ideas at the heart of it

**Chaos → symmetry is the visual thesis.** The central motion everywhere is
scatter pulling itself into order. Regulation is an *achievement*, not a passive
event — the player earns symmetry by nudging the system, and earning it pays out
a clue. As the picture resolves, the on-screen maths simplifies and the lights
cool and steady in lockstep. *(This is the seed the whole spirals/σ engine grew
from.)*

**The mismatch is the point.** Reject calm = blue, stress = red. Real inner
states conflict: outside a warm, cheerful room; inside a cold blue pulse — "I'm
having fun but I'm overwhelmed." Outside a quiet room; inside chaotic geometry —
"nobody notices the processing load." Every scene has a **surface** (what the
visitor sees) and a **hidden layer** (what the house experiences), and sometimes
the mask *slips* — a wrong rhythm, a colour bleeding, a delayed response — and
the inside leaks through the cheerful surface. That mismatch is the masking core
of the whole piece.

## 7. The rules that never bend

- **ART, NOT BLAME.** No one is named or accused on screen. Painful origins are
  hidden like puzzle clues — obscured, fictionalised into noir, discovered by
  play, never stated plainly.
- **PROVENANCE FIREWALL.** None of the on-screen prose is the author's real
  voice; it's a written costume (the "Guide"). The only genuinely personal words
  live *hidden in the source code*, never rendered on any display.
- **TRUTH-VALVE.** Every clue is tagged for how true it is; the rawest,
  most private material is quarantined — kept for a private file, never shown.

## 8. The glue — the cut test

Everything in Deep House is one thing:

> **a house trying to communicate something it cannot directly say.**

Every feature has to answer *"how does this help the house communicate?"* If it
doesn't, it's cut. That single test is the guard against the piece becoming a
museum of clever ideas (math, DJ booth, detective board, Hue lights, noir,
operator, archive) with no organism holding them together. The ending it's
building toward isn't a cure — it's **integration**: the house learning to
communicate *with itself*, ending in dynamic, living symmetry rather than
perfect stillness.

---

## 9. How the living build relates to this (honest note)

The concept above is the **north star**, not a snapshot of the current tree.
Since conception the work has spent most of its energy on the **visual organism**
— the spirals, the light engine, the organisms, and lately a phone-controller
minigame platform (see `MECHANICS-MAP.md`). Those grew straight out of §6's
chaos→symmetry thesis and §5's "earn the resolve." The detective-noir narrative
layer, the cold cases, the multi-display parallel realities, and the live
operator are designed and partly built but have had far less love than the
lights and shapes.

Read this file when a decision feels unmoored — it's the answer to "but what was
this *for*." The full, unabridged brief with every invariant is `DESIGN.md`.
