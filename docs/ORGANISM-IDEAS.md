# SPITBALL — organisms, networks, and things that flow

*Loose pool. Nothing here is worked out and nothing should be. K. asked for
adjacent ideas to build on, not a finished design.*

The slime mold hit because it's **one organism, made of flow, living on a
network, that grows by feeding and learns by merging.** So: what else has that
shape? Each entry is (a) what it really does, (b) the mechanic it hands you,
(c) what it gives you that slime mold doesn't.

`docs/SLIME-MOLD.md` is one branch worked out too early — treat it as a sample,
not the answer.

---

## A. The salt-transfer family — "acquire tolerance from something that has it"

K.'s core instinct. It shows up in nature at least five ways, and they play
*differently*.

### A1. Physarum fusion — merge, share, part
Two plasmodia fuse; the naive one gains the habituated one's salt tolerance and
keeps it after separating.
**Gives you:** temporary loss of control while merged. You are briefly two
voices in one body.

### A2. Bacterial conjugation / plasmids — the tolerance is an *object*
Bacteria extend a pilus, physically connect, and copy a plasmid across. That's
how antibiotic resistance spreads. The tolerance is a **discrete thing** that
can be carried, copied, and given away — you can still have it after giving it.
**Gives you:** trading. An economy of survivals. You could carry a tolerance
you've never needed, for someone else.

### A3. Immune memory — you earn it by surviving a small dose
No donor needed. Controlled exposure builds tolerance; overwhelming exposure
doesn't. Vaccination is literally "a survivable amount of the bad thing."
**Gives you:** a *training* verb. Deliberately dose yourself at the edge of a
hostile region, repeatedly, to build up. Rewards courage and patience, punishes
recklessness — without a fail state.

### A4. Lichen — two organisms permanently become a third
Alga + fungus. Neither survives the harsh places alone; together they colonise
bare rock. It isn't a merge-and-part; it's a **new organism** with different
abilities and a different shape.
**Gives you:** permanent partnership as a choice. Do you stay fused? You gain a
place you could never reach and you are no longer what you were.

### A5. Endosymbiosis — you swallow it and it becomes an organ
Mitochondria were free-living bacteria. Now they're part of us forever.
**Gives you:** the biggest version — absorbing another organism *becomes a
permanent new capability*, and there's no undoing it.

> **Spitball:** these could coexist. Fusion = temporary. Plasmid = tradeable.
> Immune = earned alone. Lichen = a permanent partner. Endosymbiosis = the
> endgame. That's a whole progression ladder from one idea.

## B. Networks that grow and prune

### B1. Mycorrhizal networks — the wood wide web
Fungal threads connect trees; they trade carbon and nitrogen. Big "mother" trees
feed shaded seedlings. **A dying tree dumps its carbon into the network.**
**Gives you:** *inheritance*. Something that dies gives everything it had to
what's connected to it. Also: you're not the only one being fed.

### B2. Angiogenesis — vessels grow toward what's starving
Tissue lacking oxygen releases a signal; blood vessels grow toward the signal.
**Gives you:** the field **asks to be visited**. Neglected regions emit a want,
and growth is pulled there. This is `PLAY-MODELS.md` §1's "the house wants
something" with a biological engine underneath it.

### B3. Neural pruning — you're born with too many connections
Infant brains overproduce synapses; use keeps them, disuse deletes them.
Myelination makes used paths *faster*, not just thicker.
**Gives you:** an inversion — **start connected to everything and lose what you
don't use.** The field begins as an overwhelming dense mess and *becomes* legible
through neglect. That's a gorgeous opening image and the opposite of the current
"reveal more over time."

### B4. River capture / erosion
Water carves; a deeper channel steals another's flow; whole river systems get
rerouted by one breach.
**Gives you:** sudden structural upheaval from a small cause. One connection
opens and half the network reroutes through it. Phase 3 could be a *capture
event* rather than a re-layout.

### B5. Lightning / dielectric breakdown
Charge builds invisibly; the discharge finds the path of least resistance in one
instant, and the branch pattern is the record.
**Gives you:** tension that accumulates unseen and releases all at once. Also
the most beautiful possible visual for a moment.

## C. Colonies — many small things acting as one

### C1. Dictyostelium — the *other* slime mold, and it's wilder
Solitary amoebae, living independently. When food runs out they **stream
together** into a slug, migrate as one body, then form a fruiting body in which
**~20% die to become the stalk** so the rest can escape as spores.
**Gives you:** aggregation under scarcity, and **sacrifice as a mechanic.** Part
of you must be spent, permanently, to lift the rest somewhere new.
*This is possibly a stronger fit than Physarum for the emotional register of the
piece.*

### C2. Ant stigmergy — communication by leaving traces
Ants don't talk. They modify the environment; others read the modification.
Trails strengthen with use and evaporate without it.
**Gives you:** **a firewall-perfect communication model.** The house never
speaks — it leaves marks, and marks are not prose. This might be the cleanest
solution the project has to "how does the house say things."

### C3. Quorum sensing — nothing, nothing, nothing, then everything
Bacteria release a signal molecule and stay dormant until its concentration
crosses a threshold — then the entire colony acts *simultaneously*.
**Gives you:** genuine phase transitions. Not a gate you cross, a **critical
mass that tips**. And it explains phase changes without a counter.

### C4. Murmurations / flocking
Three local rules, no leader, global shape.
**Gives you:** the field itself as a flock — stars that move together because of
local rules, not choreography.

## D. Systems that build tension and release it

### D1. Self-organised criticality — the sandpile
Grains fall one at a time; the pile self-organises to a critical slope; then
avalanches of *every size* happen, unpredictably, from identical inputs.
**Gives you:** the honest model of overload. Same small input, wildly different
consequence, and nobody's fault. For a piece about sensory load, this is almost
too on the nose.

### D2. Fermentation / sourdough starter
A living culture with a history. It changes based on where it lives and what you
feed it. **People share starters**, and a shared starter carries its origin with
it. Neglect it and it sours; it rarely dies outright.
**Gives you:** the warmest version of "everything has memory." Also the most
*domestic* — this is a **house**, and a starter is a thing that lives in a
kitchen for decades.

### D3. Crystal growth / supersaturation
Nothing happens for a long time. Then one seed, and it propagates through the
entire solution in seconds.
**Gives you:** the moment of understanding as a physical process — order
spreading through a medium that was already ready for it.

### D4. Percolation
Add connections at random to a lattice; below a threshold nothing spans it,
above it a path suddenly crosses the whole thing.
**Gives you:** connectivity as the win condition, with a sharp, mathematically
real transition. Very close to what phase 3 wants to be.

## E. Dormancy — the "not today" family

### E1. Tardigrade cryptobiosis
Expels its water, curls into a tun, survives vacuum and radiation for years,
revives with a drop of water.
**Gives you:** total safe shutdown. Nothing progresses, nothing is lost.

### E2. Sclerotium (Physarum's own dormancy) / seed dormancy
Some seeds require fire, or a cold winter, or passing through a gut before they
will germinate. **They refuse until the right thing has happened to them.**
**Gives you:** `DREAMS.md`'s refusal mechanic with a *reason* — "not today"
becomes "not until." And the condition is discoverable.

### E3. Diapause
Insects pause development based on day length, not on how they feel.
**Gives you:** the clock as a mechanic (`PLAY-MODELS.md` §2D) with a biological
alibi.

## F. Combinations worth spitballing

Loose pairings that seem to spark:

- **Neural pruning (B3) + quorum sensing (C3)** — start overwhelmingly
  connected, lose what you don't use, and phases tip when a region's remaining
  connectivity crosses threshold. The arc becomes *subtractive*.
- **Ant stigmergy (C2) + angiogenesis (B2)** — the house leaves marks toward
  what it lacks. It cannot speak but it can *want*, visibly, in the field.
- **Dictyostelium (C1) + mycorrhizal inheritance (B1)** — when starving you
  aggregate; part of you is spent to reach the new place; what's spent feeds
  what remains.
- **Sourdough (D2) + plasmids (A2)** — you keep a culture; you can *give a piece
  of it away*; what you give carries where it's been. Multi-visitor without
  networking.
- **Immune training (A3) + sandpile (D1)** — dose yourself near the hostile
  edge to build tolerance, but each dose adds a grain, and the avalanche size is
  never predictable.
- **Lichen (A4) + lightning (B5)** — a permanent partnership that changes the
  shape of the discharge when tension finally releases.

## G. Gut ranking — what I'd chase first, and why

Not a recommendation, a reaction:

1. **Dictyostelium (C1)** — aggregation and sacrifice. Emotionally the strongest
   thing in this list, and a genuinely different verb from Physarum.
2. **Ant stigmergy (C2)** — probably solves "how does the house communicate"
   permanently, and it's cheap.
3. **Neural pruning (B3)** — an inverted arc. Start drowning, become clear.
   Nothing else here restructures the whole piece so cheaply.
4. **Angiogenesis (B2)** — makes the field want things. Pairs with everything.
5. **Sourdough (D2)** — the most *house-like* idea in the pool, and the one most
   likely to survive contact with real visitors.

The salt-transfer family (A) is still the best *gate* mechanic. But it's a
component, not a game — it needs one of B/C to live inside.

---

## H. What none of these solve

Worth saying: every idea above is still a thing happening **on a screen, on a
graph**. The room, the mic, the pulse and the operator console are still
unused. `PLAY-MODELS.md` §2 is a different axis, and a mold that grows through a
*physical room* beats a mold that grows on a TV. Whichever organism wins, that
question is separate and probably more important.
